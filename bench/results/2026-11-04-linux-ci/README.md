# Results of 2026-11-04 on a Linux CI runner

The raw output of the `bench` job in CI for two pushes on the same day,
downloaded from each run's artifact. They show how the numbers in
[`../2026-11-04/`](../2026-11-04/), taken on a laptop, change on other
hardware.

## Machine

- A GitHub-hosted `ubuntu-latest` runner: Ubuntu 24.04, image
  `ubuntu-24.04` version 20261025.353.1, four virtual CPUs. A virtual
  machine shared with other work, so the numbers are noisy: the two runs
  differ by up to a third on the same row.
- The same scripts and versions as on the laptop: Umbrachyx built with
  `--release`, Valkey 9.1.3 built from its release tag, and that release's
  `valkey-benchmark` as the client for both.
- Synthetic data only, generated by `valkey-benchmark`.

## Files

| Folder | Commit | Scripts |

| `run-1` | `4f38777` | `bench/umbrachyx.sh`, `bench/valkey.sh` |

Latencies are in milliseconds. `valkey-benchmark` reports them in coarse
buckets, so small values are rounded.

## What differs from the laptop

- A sync to disk is far cheaper here: one durable `SET` takes about
  0.3 ms, against 4 ms on the laptop. So Valkey's `GET`, which waits for
  the sync of the writes around it, waits less, and Umbrachyx's lead there
  shrinks from about 650 times to 20 to 28 times (medians).
- A `GET` from one client costs the same on both servers here, where on
  the laptop Umbrachyx answered about 3.5 times sooner.
- With one durable writer, and with 50 clients reading at once, Valkey is
  ahead here: about 1.4 to 1.5 times the writes and 1.4 to 1.7 times the
  reads.
- Linux lets a process start far more threads than macOS, so Umbrachyx
  serves 10,000 connections. In `run-2/connections.csv` its slowest reads
  (p99) are 0.4 to 0.6 times Valkey's at every size.
